Physical Model Formulations & Mathematical Equations

1. Low Supersaturation Scenario vs Spinodal Decomposition

At 10 mM CaCO3 (ND-i-003), the reaction solution resides below the spinodal boundary. Unlike high-supersaturation regimes (e.g. 40 mM in ND-i-001/ND-i-009) where barrierless phase separation produces a bicontinuous mass fractal gel network ($D_m \approx 1.84$), ND-i-003 exhibits classical homogeneous nucleation of discrete, non-percolating amorphous calcium carbonate (ACC) nanospheres.

2. Unified Multi-Component Scattering Model

$$I_{\rm tot}(q) = I_{\rm sphere}(q) + I_{\rm droplets}(q) + I_{\rm bkg}$$ $$I_{\rm droplets}(q) = (\Delta\rho)^2 \int_0^\infty n_{\rm large}(r) V(r)^2 P(q, r)\, dr \xrightarrow{q \gg 1/R_{\rm large}} \frac{27 \phi_{\rm large}}{8\pi R_{\rm large} q^4} = \frac{A_{\rm porod}}{q^4}$$ Macroscopic droplets ($R_{\rm large} \approx 2.1 - 2.8\ \mu\mathrm{m}$, $\phi_{\rm large} \approx (5.0 - 6.0) \times 10^{-5}$) represent mixing/shear-induced emulsion droplets identical to the phase observed in ND-i-001 and ND-i-009. This form factor captures the low-$q$ forward scattering rollover ($q < 0.005\text{ nm}^{-1}$) avoiding unphysical $1/q^4$ divergence, while smoothly decaying as $q^{-4}$ at higher $q$.

3. Polydisperse Sphere Form Factor

$$I_{\rm sphere}(q) = (\Delta\rho)^2 \int_0^\infty n(r) V(r)^2 P(q, r)\, dr$$ $$P(q, r) = \left[ \frac{3 \left(\sin(qr) - qr \cos(qr)\right)}{(qr)^3} \right]^2, \quad V(r) = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$$

4. Lognormal Size Distribution

$$n(r) = \frac{\phi}{\langle V \rangle} \frac{1}{r \sigma_{\rm log} \sqrt{2\pi}} \exp\left( - \frac{(\ln r - \mu_{\rm log})^2}{2\sigma_{\rm log}^2} \right)$$ $$\mu_{\rm log} = \ln\left(\frac{\langle R \rangle^2}{\sqrt{\sigma_R^2 + \langle R \rangle^2}}\right), \quad \sigma_{\rm log} = \sqrt{\ln\left(1 + \frac{\sigma_R^2}{\langle R \rangle^2}\right)}$$

5. Porod Law for Compact 3D Objects with Sharp Interfaces

$$\lim_{q \gg 1/\langle R \rangle} I_{\rm sphere}(q) = 2\pi (\Delta\rho)^2 S_{\rm spec} q^{-4} \quad (D = 3)$$ The observation of strict $q^{-4}$ decay across $q \in [0.03, 0.10]\text{ nm}^{-1}$ confirms that the particles are smooth, dense 3D spheres rather than diffuse mass fractals ($q^{-D_m}$ with $D_m < 3$).

6. Stoichiometric Precursor Mass Balance

$$\phi_{\rm max} = \frac{[\text{Ca}^{2+}]_0 \cdot M_{\text{CaCO}_3}}{\rho_{\text{ACC}}} = \frac{0.010\text{ mol/L} \times 100.09\text{ g/mol}}{2.0\text{ g/cm}^3 \times 1000\text{ cm}^3/\text{L}} \approx 5.0 \times 10^{-4}$$ The fitted volume fraction $\phi(t)$ plateaus at $(4.41 \pm 0.03) \times 10^{-4}$, representing near complete conversion towards the stoichiometric theoretical maximum $\phi_{\rm max} \approx 5.0 \times 10^{-4}$.
