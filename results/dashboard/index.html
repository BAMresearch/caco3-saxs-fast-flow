Role of the viscoelastic mesh in the context of prenucleation clusters (PNCs)

In the non-classical nucleation framework, hydrated calcium and carbonate ions form dynamic coordination oligomers or prenucleation clusters (PNCs) with characteristic dimensions \(\sim 0.6 - 2.0\text{ nm}\). In dilute sub-spinodal solutions, these species remain in dynamic equilibrium with free ions without forming small-angle scattering excess.

Upon crossing the spinodal boundary into liquid-liquid phase separation (LLPS), the dense hydrated liquid precursor phase rapidly concentrates the ions (local concentration \(\sim 5 - 10\text{ M}\)). Under these concentrated conditions, PNCs do not persist as isolated globular particles with sharp boundaries; instead, their hydration shells overlap and interpenetrate, forming a transient, dynamically entangled network or viscoelastic mesh.

The dynamic concentration fluctuation component in the SAXS model: \[ I_{\rm fluc}(q) = \frac{A_{\rm fluc}}{\left[ 1 + \frac{1}{6} (q \xi_{\rm fluc})^2 \right]^2} \] directly measures the mesh size (mean coordination distance between entangled PNC nodes) \(\xi_{\rm fluc} \approx 1.5 - 3.0\text{ nm}\). The Tanaka decay exponent \(\mu = 4.0\) reflects continuous concentration gradients without sharp dielectric phase boundaries. As the system ages and expels water, this viscoelastic mesh structurally arrests and vitrifies into the primary amorphous calcium carbonate building blocks (\(\langle R_{\rm prim} \rangle \approx 2.8 - 6.5\text{ nm}\)) that constitute the mesoscale mass fractal gel.

Concentration axis in pure CaCO₃ (10 → 15 → 20 → 40 mM)

Varying reactant concentration in pure CaCO₃ establishes a sharp boundary at C* ≈ 12 – 15 mM:

  • 10.0 mM (ND-i-003, sub-spinodal regime): Precipitation proceeds via classical nucleation and growth of discrete, non-percolating amorphous calcium carbonate (ACC) nanospheres. Small-angle scattering displays no mass fractal structure factor (S(q) ≡ 1). Scattering at q ∈ [0.005, 0.15] nm-1 is strictly governed by the polydisperse sphere form factor with asymptotic Porod q-4 decay (D = 3, sharp interface). The short-distance camera (3.0 m) detects flat solvent background without colloidal building block excess.
  • 15.0 mM (ND-i-004, crossover phase boundary): At 15 mM, the system resides directly on the spinodal phase boundary. The scattering profile requires a hybrid model: simultaneous presence of primary building blocks (⟨Rprim⟩ ≈ 4.94 nm) assembling into an early percolated mass fractal network (Dm = 2.85, ξ = 108.7 nm) coexisting with a population of discrete spherical ACC nanospheres (⟨Racc⟩ ≈ 47.7 nm) generating form-factor oscillations in the 3.0 m camera.
  • 20.0 mM (ND-i-002, spinodal gelation): Above the boundary, spinodal decomposition dominates. Primary spherical nodes (⟨Rprim⟩ ≈ 11.2 nm) link into a space-filling mass fractal network with Df = 1.86 and correlation length ξ = 356 nm. Scattering at lowest q (< 0.005 nm-1) transitions to Porod surface scattering (Aporod / q4), indicating the onset of coarsening dense domains. Discrete ACC spheres are absent.
  • 40.0 mM (ND-i-009, primary pure CaCO₃ benchmark): Primary benchmark with 351 frames (15 ms/frame). Resolves the pre-percolation induction phase (frame 0: ξ = 46.7 nm) smoothly transitioning into an infinite percolated network (ξ → ∞) with continuous fractal dimension drift Df(t) ∈ [1.881, 1.925]. Simultaneously resolves the PNC viscoelastic fluctuation mesh (ξfluc ≈ 2.72 → 2.84 nm) and secondary droplet coarsening (2.27 → 3.20 μm).
  • 40.0 mM at pH 11.3 (ND-i-001, rapid quench counterpart): At elevated pH 11.3, higher free carbonate activity accelerates demixing, resulting in an instantaneous barrierless quench (tind < 0.06 s) into a dense bicontinuous network (Df = 2.33, ξ = 735 nm, ⟨Rprim⟩ = 3.50 nm) with pinched droplets (⟨Rlarge⟩ = 3.01 μm).

Additive perturbation branch (Co²⁺ dopant, ND-i-005)

ND-i-005 is the sole reaction series containing a foreign chemical additive. The addition of cobalt(II) modifies interfacial thermodynamics:

  • Enlargement of primary nodes: Cobalt adsorption lowers interfacial tension γ, shifting the primary building block radius to 6.59 nm.
  • Extended correlation cutoff: Network correlation cutoff expands to ξ = 1,190 nm with a more open fractal geometry (Df = 2.10).
  • Fluctuation enhancement: Elevated viscoelastic fluctuation amplitude confirms altered hydration dynamics within the dense coordination network.

Viscoelastic phase separation (VPS) framework verification

The experimental trajectories and model transitions verify the mathematical framework of Viscoelastic phase separation (VPS) formulated by Tanaka (2000, 2001) and detailed in Mathematical framework for VPS scattering:

  • Dynamic asymmetry (α >> 0): Hydrated calcium-carbonate ion oligomers and DOLLOPs exhibit slow relaxation timescales (τp >> τs) relative to the bulk water solvent, generating strong viscoelastic stress coupling during phase separation.
  • Viscoelastic length scale (ξve): The crossover scale ξve ∼ √(K + 4/3 G)/ζ τ separates elastic suppression at short wavelengths (q ξve > 1) from hydrodynamic demixing at large wavelengths (q ξve < 1).
  • Volume shrinking of the transient gel: In the 40 mM pure benchmark (ND-i-009), the network strands densify continuously (Dm drifts from 1.881 to 1.925) whilst primary unit radius remains invariant (Rprim ≈ 2.80 nm). This quantitative result confirms solvent expulsion from the viscoelastic network.
  • Phase inversion and droplet coexistence: Mechanical stress relaxation along contracting struts leads to localized rupture and capillary pinch-off, shedding compact macroscopic liquid droplets (⟨Rlarge⟩ ≈ 2.2 to 3.5 μm, φlarge ≈ 10-4) that coexist with the space-filling gel mesh.
  • Distinction from Arrested Spinodal Decomposition (ASD): In contrast to ASD in hard colloidal suspensions where structure factors freeze permanently via glass jamming, the calcium carbonate system shows continuous kinetic evolution, volume shrinking, and phase inversion.

Multi-scale physical scattering formalism
The total coherent scattering intensity \(I_{\rm tot}(q)\) across the momentum transfer range \(q \in [0.002, 2.5]\text{ nm}^{-1}\) is modelled as the linear superposition of decoupled structural domains:
\[ I_{\rm tot}(q) = I_{\rm droplet/ACC}(q) + I_{\rm network}(q) + I_{\rm fluc}(q) + I_{\rm bkg} \]

1. Primary mass fractal network

Formed by polydisperse spherical building blocks following a lognormal size distribution \(n(R)\) with Teixeira mass fractal inter-particle structure factor \(S(q, D_m, \xi)\):

\[ I_{\rm network}(q) = (\Delta\rho)^2 \int_0^\infty n(R) V^2(R) P(q, R) S(q, R, D_m, \xi) \, dR \] \[ S(q, R, D_m, \xi) = 1 + \frac{D_m \Gamma(D_m - 1)}{(q R)^{D_m}} \frac{\sin[(D_m - 1) \arctan(q \xi)]}{\left[1 + (q \xi)^{-2}\right]^{(D_m - 1)/2}} \]

2. Capillary emulsion droplets and discrete ACC nanospheres

Spherical particles (either macroscopic dense liquid droplets at low q or discrete ACC nanospheres at intermediate q) with lognormal size distribution:

\[ I_{\rm sphere}(q) = (\Delta\rho)^2 \int_0^\infty n(R) V^2(R) \left[ 3 \frac{\sin(qR) - qR\cos(qR)}{(qR)^3} \right]^2 dR \]

3. Dynamic viscoelastic concentration fluctuations and the PNC mesh

Internal concentration fluctuations of the dense viscoelastic network with correlation length \(\xi_{\rm fluc}\) and Porod surface-decay exponent \(\mu = 4.0\) (Tanaka / Fisher-Burford formulation):

\[ I_{\rm fluc}(q) = \frac{A_{\rm fluc}}{\left[ 1 + \frac{1}{6} (q \xi_{\rm fluc})^2 \right]^2} \]

Physically, this fluctuation term represents the transient viscoelastic mesh formed by entangled, hydrated prenucleation clusters (PNCs) within the dense liquid precursor phase. The correlation length \(\xi_{\rm fluc} \approx 1.5 - 3.0\text{ nm}\) directly corresponds to the mesh spacing between ionic coordination cross-links prior to solid ACC vitrification.

4. Asymptotic Porod interface

Scattering from macroscopic phase-separated interfaces satisfying Porod's universal law (\(I \propto q^{-4}\)):

\[ I_{\rm porod}(q) = \frac{A_{\rm porod}}{q^4} \]
